AI in medicine: approvals and evidence

Since 2018, FDA clearances of AI-enabled devices grew 5.2 times, from 65 to 335 a year. Published AI randomized trials grew 5.1 times over the same period, from 82 to 420. The two lines move together. What stands out is where the devices sit: 96% came through the 510(k) route, and 76% are radiology.

What this does not show. The trials counted here are about AI in medicine in general. They are not trials of the devices on the FDA list, and open data does not link the two. So this page cannot say how many cleared devices have trial evidence behind them.

Known limits. PubMed counts rely on MeSH indexing, which lags for recent papers, so the latest years are likely undercounted. The FDA calls its list periodic and not comprehensive. A 510(k) clearance shows a device is substantially equivalent to an earlier one and usually does not need a clinical trial.
